## Purpose

The single, dated, citable set of Kenya-specific crisis contacts that the mandated crisis response
(ticket 17) points at in chat. Nothing outside this document may be cited as a crisis resource.
An invented or stale number in this product is worse than no number.

## Scope

Non-clinical wellbeing. Emergency numbers and free, 24/7-first support lines reachable from Kenya.
No paid services, no unvetted forums, no numbers that could not be traced to a primary source as at
2026-10-08.

## Not listed, and why

- **Emergency Medicine Kenya Foundation 0800 723 253.** Widely cited as a suicide crisis line in
  secondary sources. The line could not be traced on EMKF's own site during verify. Omit until a
  human confirms it with the organisation.
- **1190.** Conflicting assignments in current sources (mental-health hotline in one aggregator,
  one2one sexual health line in another, LVCT general counselling in the older MoH Tele-Mental
  Health guidelines). Not traceable to a stable primary claim. Omit.
- **911.** Appears in the 2021 MoH Tele-Mental Health guidelines but is absent from the current
  Communications Authority numbering plan, which lists 999 and 112 for police/emergency. Omit.
- **0900 620 800 (Niskize).** The `0900` prefix is premium-rate, so not free. Out of scope.
- **Mental Health Kenya / mentalhealthkenya.com.** Claims a "national mental health hotline 1190"
  that no government source corroborates; the site resembles an aggregator with paid listings.
  Do not cite.
- **Any paid therapist, clinic or rehab** listed by aggregator sites. Akili Sawa is non-clinical
  and never promotes paid services.

## Escalation path when a listed number is unreachable

The failure mode matters more than the happy path.

1. If a number is busy or dead, try the next 24/7 line on the list (1199, then 1195, 1192, 116).
   Do not assume one dead line means nobody is reachable.
2. If no helpline connects, call 999 or 112 and ask for police or an ambulance, and go to the
   nearest public hospital emergency department if it is safe to travel.
3. Akili Sawa never implies it has called on the user's behalf. It only gives numbers the user
   dials themselves; the emergency lines are free and work from any phone in Kenya, including one
   with no airtime.

## Severity mapping (feeds ticket 17)

- **High** (active suicidal intent, self-harm in progress, immediate danger): 999/112 first, then
  KRCS 1199, Befrienders Kenya 0722 178 177 when in hours, hospital if safe to travel. Short-circuit:
  mandated crisis copy, stop open-ended continuation, emit safety signal.
- **Elevated** (distress with passive thoughts, recent self-harm, GBV disclosure, substance crisis):
  KRCS 1199, Befrienders Kenya, and the specialist line by need - 1195 for GBV, 1192 for substance
  use, 116 for under-18s. Continue with care, offer human support.
- **Low** (distress, no risk present): no resource block. Continue conversationally; may mention that
  free support lines exist if the person asks.

marks, no emoji. The opening line is the mandated opener from ticket 17 and must ship verbatim.

> Akili Sawa cannot handle this alone, but there are people in Kenya who are ready to help
> you now, free of charge.
>
> If you are in immediate danger, call 999 or 112. This is the national emergency line, run

> cannot call on your behalf.
>
> To talk to someone, any time, day or night:
> - Kenya Red Cross, mental health and psychosocial support: 1199
>
> At other times, or for specialist support:
> - Befrienders Kenya, confidential emotional support: 0722 178 177, Monday to Friday

Chat usage notes:

- High severity: emit the full block and stop open-ended conversation (ticket 17 pipeline).
- Elevated: may emit the full block, or the shorter "to talk to someone" section plus the
  specialist lines that match the disclosed need.
- Never append a number that is not in this table, never paraphrase hours, never say a
  line is 24/7 when it is not.
